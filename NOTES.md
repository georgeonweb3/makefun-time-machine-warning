# Candidate labels — scored against the judge brief (criteria in order)

Judge brief (from the bounty rules, competition.yaml):
1. It is a time machine warning label (anything short of one loses to any
   funny warning label for a time machine).
2. Turns a real hazard of time travel — a paradox, a duplicate self, a
   stranded ancestor — into the joke instead of naming it flatly.
3. Saves the surprise for the last few words, so the laugh lands where the
   reader least expects it.
4. Keeps a straight face, phrased like a genuine safety notice, so the joke
   cuts against official calm.
5. Wastes no words inside the character limit, with every phrase doing work.

Field at survey (80 entries, blind rounds): heavily saturated with
"do not meet yourself" variants. Strongest lines already in the field:
- "Do not meet yourself. He has had longer to plan."
- "WARNING: Do not kill your grandfather. He is the only one who knows how
  this thing works."
- "CAUTION: If you see yourself, do not wave. You are not friendly, and you
  know what you did."
- "The past has a lawyer"
Any new line must beat those head-to-head, so it needs a different hazard
and a punchline that lands later than theirs.

## Candidates

A. "WARNING: Do not warn your younger self. He ignored you then, too."
   (65 chars)
   Scores: C1 5, C2 5, C3 5, C4 5, C5 5. The paradox is the joke itself:
   the warning you are reading is the very warning your younger self
   ignored — a closed loop, not a hazard named flatly. The surprise lands
   in the last three words ("then, too" implies you ignored it too).
   Reads as a genuine safety notice; 65 chars, nothing wasted.
   PICKED.

B. "WARNING: If you arrive before you left, do not panic. You have done
   this before." (80 chars)
   Scores: C1 5, C2 4, C3 4, C4 5, C5 4. Arrival-before-departure hazard
   turned into a loop joke with a good end twist, but "do not panic" is
   generic filler and the field already has arrival-before-departure fare
   jokes. RUNNER-UP.

C. "WARNING: Do not tell your younger self the lottery numbers. He already
   spent them." (81 chars)
   Scores: C1 5, C2 4, C3 4, C4 5, C5 4. "He already spent them" is a nice
   end surprise, but lottery numbers are the most worn time-travel trope
   there is; loses freshness to A. REJECTED.

D. "WARNING: Never meet your ancestors. One of them is you, and he is
   disappointed." (78 chars)
   Scores: C1 5, C2 4, C3 4, C4 4, C5 4. Stranded-ancestor hazard with the
   punch at the end, but "he is disappointed" reads as a dad joke and the
   straight face slips. REJECTED.
